A Lean proof is a sequence of tactic steps, each of which turns one goal state
into the next. We ask whether these trajectories move between areas of
mathematics in a regular way that a small model can capture. We embed goal
states, cluster them into microstates, fit a Markov state model with proof
steps as the unit of time, and coarse-grain it into metastable sets with PCCA+.
Against MSC 2020 labels from two LLM judges, the metastable sets reach an NMI
of \resMacroNMI, compared with \resFolderNMI{} for a baseline that labels each proof
by its Mathlib folder, and the slowest area-switching process relaxes over
about \resSlowestTimescale{} tactic steps. Used as a prior in best-first search with
DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5, the area model changes the number of problems solved
under a fixed budget by \resSearchGainMiniF{} on miniF2F and \resSearchGainPutnam{} on PutnamBench.
