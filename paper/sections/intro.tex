\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Formal proofs often cross areas of mathematics. An inequality from analysis
may close with an algebraic identity, and a number theory goal may reduce to
a counting argument. A human prover notices these shifts and uses them: once
a goal looks combinatorial, the right lemmas and tactics change. Neural
provers for Lean \citep{yang2023leandojo,xin2025deepseekproverv15} have no
explicit notion of area. They sample tactics from a language model and expand
the most likely branches, so any knowledge of where a proof is heading lives
implicitly in the model's weights.

We study area changes directly, borrowing a tool from molecular simulation.
A Markov state model (MSM) describes a long trajectory through a
high-dimensional space as jumps between a modest number of discrete states,
and identifies sets of states the trajectory stays in for long stretches
before switching \citep{prinz2011markov,husic2018markov}. In molecular
dynamics the trajectory is a sequence of conformations and the metastable
sets are conformational states. Here the trajectory is the sequence of goal
states in a proof, the clock is the proof step, and our hypothesis is that
the metastable sets correspond to areas of mathematics.

This framing gives testable claims. If proofs move between areas in a
regular way, a Markov model at some lag $\lag$ should predict transitions at
multiples of $\lag$ (the Chapman--Kolmogorov test), its slow implied
timescales should stop changing as $\lag$ grows, and its metastable sets
should agree with independent area labels better than a simple baseline.
Mathematical areas carry no labels in Lean or Mathlib, so we label proofs
with top-level MSC 2020 codes \citep{msc2020} from two LLM judges and measure
how far the judges agree before trusting them. If the model holds up, it
offers a signal for search: which area a candidate tactic leads to, and
whether the search has been stuck in one area for too long.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A Markov state model over Lean goal-state trajectories, built from
  LeanDojo's traced Mathlib proofs \citep{yang2023leandojo}, with every
  standard MSM check reported (implied-timescale convergence,
  Chapman--Kolmogorov, connectivity, bootstrap error bars).
  \item A comparison of the model's metastable sets with MSC 2020 labels from
  two LLM judges, against a baseline that labels each proof by its Mathlib
  folder.
  \item An area-aware prior for best-first proof search, evaluated on
  miniF2F \citep{zheng2022minif2f} and ProofNet \citep{azerbayev2023proofnet}
  under a fixed budget.
\end{itemize}
